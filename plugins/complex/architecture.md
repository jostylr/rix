# Complex and Cayley–Dickson plugin architecture

The numerical tower is split by mathematical responsibility rather than by
surface syntax.

```text
.numerics
    certified algorithms returning real singletons

.complex
    two real-singleton components
    rectangular/disc enclosures
    branch-aware complex functions

.cayley
    scalar-generic Cayley–Dickson component algebra

.quaternion / .octonion
    typed façades, division conventions, and intrinsic functions
```

## Why Complex is not an EnclosableReal

An `EnclosableReal` returns one ordered rational interval. Complex numbers have
no compatible total order, so `ComplexReal` publishes
`rix.enclosable-complex@1` and returns two rational component intervals. This
avoids weakening the real contract or hiding an arbitrary ordering choice.

The component contract is intentionally the already implemented certified,
arbitrarily refinable singleton protocol. Same-family operations remain in
their family and different families meet at Oracle. Finite real enclosures are
sets and belong in a future `ComplexRegion` rather than `ComplexReal`.

## Cayley–Dickson responsibilities

`.cayley` will parameterize component arithmetic over a central commutative
real-scalar service. It owns basis layout, recursive multiplication,
conjugation, component boxes, norm evidence, and explicit left/right division
operations. The current rational-only implementation in `exact-algebras`
remains valid and will become one provider or adapter rather than being
silently widened.

`.quaternion` can safely add intrinsic functions based on `q=a+v` and the
two-dimensional associative slice generated by `1` and `v/|v|`. It must not
apply commutative rewrites to products of independent quaternion values.

`.octonion` may reuse one-variable real-coefficient power series because the
subalgebra generated by one octonion is associative. General products remain
nonassociative, so stored parentheses and evaluation order are semantic.

The generic tower stops making division-algebra promises after octonions.
Later Cayley–Dickson algebras have zero divisors and a non-composition norm;
their capabilities must omit general inverse/division even if the component
container is structurally similar.

## Function policy

Functions are divided into three levels:

1. Entire real-coefficient functions such as exponential, sine, and cosine can
   use component identities or direct validated series.
2. Logarithms, roots, arbitrary powers, inverse trigonometric functions, and
   branch-sensitive special functions return explicit branch results.
3. Continuation-dependent and multivalued functions require path or branch-set
   values rather than one silently selected scalar.

Rectangles are the Phase 1 interchange geometry. Complex discs, unions,
Taylor models, shared-expression correlation, and direct complex special
function kernels are later optimization and topology layers; none may return
an enclosure smaller than the facts certify.
